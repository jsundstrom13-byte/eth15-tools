Sigma is the gap between the perp-implied index and the target divided by the typical move left before settlement, so it drifts outward on its own as time runs out; a falling line means the index is moving toward the target faster than that. The slope is fitted to the gap itself and converted with the current sigma. The state line is green while Up holds, red while Down holds, amber for Flip when the fitted pace crosses the target before the close, and red Flipped for two minutes after a crossing.

Each lot keeps its own price and fee. Hold, sell and cut above are judged on the whole position at its average cost. Shares sold can be fewer than you hold: the rest stays open. The first lot stays editable in the shares and price boxes above; remove a lot here if it was typed by mistake.

Prices are for the called side. Buy is fair value less the fee and a 1¢ margin. Push is break even: the lowest whole-cent sale that returns the full cost of the position after the sale fee, with the chance the contract gets there before last exit. Sell target and cut are the contract's fair value after a one-sigma BTC move for or against you over the next few minutes; the BTC levels are where that move lands. If a typed ask is at or under the buy price, the call says buy now. Max target is the resting limit sell that maximises expected gain: the price the contract reaches after a k-sigma BTC move in your favour, weighted by the chance such a move is touched before last exit (about 2 × (1 − Φ(k)) over the next few minutes). It is the stretch price worth asking for, not the price to wait for.

Predictor track record

Each window's next-round call is saved twice: provisional at 5 minutes before the open, and firm at the open. It is scored when that window settles, about 90 seconds after its close. 96 windows a day; the intervals need about 30. Kept in this browser.

How Kalshi BTC 15 min settles

The target is the average of the CF Benchmarks BTC index over the 60 seconds before the window opens. The result is the same 60-second average before the window closes. Up wins if the result is above the target. Checked three times: for 10:00 AM ET this page computed 2,707.4435 and Kalshi posted 2,707.4447; for 10:15, 2,701.6018 against 2,701.6038. With 3 seconds left in the 10:15 window, the projected result was 2,678.036 and the settlement came in at 2,678.032. The index comes from Kalshi's public price file, the one its own chart reads. In each window's final minute that file's per-second series switches to the forming average, so this page rebuilds the raw index from the averages, exact to 0.0004 dollars.

What would make it an edge

A few seconds' lead only pays if Kalshi's quotes are slower than the perp. Kalshi's market makers can buy the index directly at one to five updates a second, without the public file's delay, and most watch perps. Whether the Up and Down prices lag can't be measured without Kalshi order-book data, which needs an API key. The model and the fee column show whether a typed ask leaves room after the fee.
